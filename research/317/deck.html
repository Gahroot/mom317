The short version

Night hero, morning page: gold 3:17 in lamp light, then warm sand sections — with one solid button, email only.

01

It fixes the colour problem

Gold on sand is 2.06:1 contrast. That fails on screen. On near‑black the same gold is 6.4–9.1:1.

02

The name becomes the hero

3:17 is a moment. The first screen is that moment: dark room, warm lamp, amber bottle on the nightstand.

03

Reading stays easy

The audience is 45+. Everything after the hero is dark ink on parchment (15.6:1) at 18px body size.

04

It's how luxury converts

Aesop, Prose, Function and Aplós keep the page quiet and make one solid button loud. No hype colours, no ghost buttons.

The page moves from night to morning as you scroll, which acts out the brand vision's before‑and‑after: exhausted, frustrated, alone → rested, energised, hopeful.

Three directions

Apothecary, Night‑to‑Morning, or The Letter

A · Light

The Apothecary

Aesop · Function · Prose

Parchment page, specimen‑style amber bottle, charcoal type, one deep‑amber button.

  • Most legible and safest
  • Fastest to build
  • Gold logo must darken; looks like many brands
B · Recommended

3:17 a.m. — Night to Morning

Aplós structure · Amrit palette · Aesop lighting · Function button rule

Lamp‑lit dark hero with the gold wordmark; the page moves into sand and parchment as you scroll.

  • Ownable; the gold works
  • Tells the before→after story with no words
  • Keep dark areas short; photography is make‑or‑break
C · Editorial

The Letter

Alison Roman · Savor · Hers message order

Opens like a letter: "For fifteen years, I woke at 3:17." Bookish serif, reddish‑brown button.

  • Most emotionally honest; strong for faith story
  • Great for warm traffic (her network)
  • Slower to show product; weaker on cold ads

Recommendation: build B and use C's founder letter as section 3. If launch traffic is mostly her own community, A/B test B against C.

Page blueprint

Twelve sections, one job each. The button follows every doubt it removes.

0Announcement bar · honest urgency: founding list, ship month, batch size
1Hero · night · headline, subhead, email + button, proof line, lamp‑lit bottle, no nav ● CTA
2Recognition · "You're not imagining it." Her symptoms in her words Hers
3Founder letter · 15 years, portrait, signature ● CTA
4The ritual · 3 numbered steps on dark amber Prose
5What's inside · cards with exact mg + "what's not inside" ticker ● CTA
6Proof · testers (name, age, stage), advisor note; stats only if real
7Founding benefits · first batch · locked price · founder's note · a say in what's next ● CTA
8The 3:17 system · Cool, Calm, Travel, Restore marked "in development"
9Mission · one million rested women; giving; one quiet faith line
10FAQ · grogginess, habit, HRT/meds, ship date, price, refund
11Final CTA · night · "Tonight, when you wake at 3:17, be ready." ● CTA
12Footer · FDA disclaimer, privacy, contact

Scroll rhythm: night → morning → a dark‑amber ritual band → sand benefits → night again for the last ask.

Faith, with care

Let "Approved by God" shape the tone (service, stewardship, truth) and say it once, sincerely, in the founder letter or mission section.

For the founder to confirm: Zephaniah 3:17 reads in part "he will rest in his love" (KJV). If the name was chosen partly for this, it's a beautiful quiet layer for the letter or inside the label. The brand vision doesn't mention it, so ask first.

3:17 · Waitlist research15
Measure and test

Aim for 8–20% of visitors signing up. Test the biggest lever first.

Track from day one

  • Visit → signup (the number that matters)
  • Signup → quiz completed
  • Signup → shared, and referrals per sharer
  • By traffic source and by phone vs desktop

Benchmarks from the earlier report: cold traffic averages 2–5%; good pages reach 8–20%; warm community traffic 25%+.

Test order

  • 1 · Headline: the moment vs the founder
  • 2 · Night hero (B) vs light apothecary hero (A)
  • 3 · "Join the founding list" vs "Reserve my place"
  • 4 · Signup count shown vs hidden (once it's meaningful)
  • 5 · Founding benefits above vs below ingredients

Never test fake scarcity or fake counters. It breaks "truthful in our words".

Before launch, have a lawyer check health claims, referral rules and email consent. Full notes: research/317/3-17-waitlist-research.md and the earlier Sleep Shot report §8.
